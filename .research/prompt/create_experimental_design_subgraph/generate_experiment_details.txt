# Experiment Strategy
Experiment 1: Cross-Domain Few-Shot Benchmarking
-------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that a small mixture of learned sub-spaces plus episode–level gating raises accuracy and calibration on naturally heterogeneous tasks.

Protocol
1. Datasets
   • Meta-Dataset (MD): use the official train set (8 domains) for meta-training; test separately on (i) in-distribution MD domains, (ii) the two MD unseen domains (Traffic-Signs, MS-COCO), and (iii) three held-out domains (MNIST, CUB, Cars) to measure domain shift.
   • miniImageNet and tieredImageNet in the standard 5-way 1/5-shot setting for direct comparison with prior work.
2. Methods
   • MoSS-HyP (K∈{1,2,4,8}, r=32).
   • Ablations: (a) single basis (K=1), (b) vector → scalar β, (c) no OOD fallback, (d) no Grassmann repulsion.
   • External baselines: ProtoNet, CoRe-HyP, FaCT-HyP, LFTNet, FeCAM.
3. Metrics
   • Acc@1 and 95 % CI.
   • Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins).
   • Episode-level negative-log-likelihood (NLL).
   • Computational cost: average wall-clock time per episode on A100 and on a Pixel 7 CPU.
4. Evaluation
   10 000 random episodes per split.  Report macro mean across domains and the full per-domain table.

Expected Outcome
   • On unseen and held-out MD domains, MoSS-HyP(K=4) beats FaCT-HyP by ≈7 pp accuracy and ≈35 % lower ECE; gains on mini/tieredImageNet are smaller but consistent (≈1 pp).  
   • K=1 ablation reverts to FaCT-HyP numbers, evidencing the benefit of multiple bases.  
   • Removing vector β hurts ECE badly (≈2×).  
   • Latency grows <3 % from K=1→8, confirming complexity is independent of K.
   These results would demonstrate that MoSS-HyP captures domain-specific anisotropy while preserving efficiency.

Experiment 2: Controlled Sub-Space Shift Simulation
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Provide a clean, repeatable test showing that mixture-of-sub-spaces adapts when the true class covariances lie in different low-rank directions.

Protocol
1. Data Generation
   • Start from ImageNet-1k pretrained conv-net features (d=2048) for 600 random ImageNet classes.
   • For each episode, sample a rank-r (r=32) orthonormal matrix T and generate synthetic ‘views’ by projecting features:  f′ = f + TΛ½ϵ, ϵ∼𝒩(0,I_r).  Draw T from one of M latent bases (M=6).  Some episodes share T; others receive a novel T to emulate OOD.
   • Vary (i) alignment angle between latent T’s, (ii) proportion of unseen T’s (0–40 %).
2. Methods/Settings
   Same model list as Exp-1.
3. Metrics
   • Acc@1, ECE and NLL.  
   • Episodic FPR95 when the episode uses an unseen T (OOD detection via entropy of α).
4. Evaluation Grid
   5 alignment levels × 5 OOD ratios, 2 000 episodes each.

Expected Outcome
   • When latent T matches one of the learned bases, MoSS-HyP accuracy ≈ upper bound (oracle).  
   • As alignment angle widens, single-basis FaCT-HyP accuracy degrades sharply (>10 pp), whereas MoSS-HyP degrades gracefully (<3 pp until 45°).  
   • For unseen T episodes, entropy H(α) rises, triggering fallback; FPR95 drops >40 % vs FaCT-HyP.  
   The synthetic control isolates sub-space mismatch and proves that the mixture/gating machinery is the critical ingredient.

Experiment 3: Scalability & Mobile Inference Study
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Verify that the method meets the practical constraint of cost O(dr²) and that computation is independent of K and number of queries.

Protocol
1. Hardware
   • Training: 8×A100 (80 GB) cluster.  
   • Inference: (a) single A100 GPU, (b) Google Pixel 7 CPU.
2. Variables
   • Episode rank r ∈ {8,16,32,64}.
   • Basis count K ∈ {1,2,4,8,16} (fixed r=32).
   • Number of queries per episode q ∈ {1,5,20,100}.
3. Workloads
   • Use episodes from the real-world personal-photo benchmark (Sec. Setup).
   • Measure: (i) forward-pass wall-clock time, (ii) peak RAM/VRAM, (iii) energy on Pixel 7 (Android BatteryStats API).
   • Ensure identical batch sizes and compiler flags (PyTorch 2.0 with torch.compile).

Expected Outcome
   • Time ∝ r² and ∝ q; curves for different K almost overlap (≤4 % spread), empirically validating O(dr²) independence from K.  
   • With r=32, MoSS-HyP needs ≈11 ms per 5-way episode on Pixel 7 vs 10 ms for FaCT-HyP; energy overhead <5 %.  
   • Memory overhead <2 MB per extra basis, making K=16 still fit inside 256 MB RAM budget.  
   This confirms that the richer model incurs negligible extra cost, satisfying on-device deployment constraints.

Taken together, the three experiments demonstrate: (1) superior real-world accuracy/calibration across heterogeneous domains, (2) the causal link between mixture-of-sub-spaces and robustness via a controlled study, and (3) practical scalability that justifies the method in production settings.
