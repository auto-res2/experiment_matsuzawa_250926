# Experiment Strategy
--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1 – Canonical & Cross-Domain Few-Shot Benchmark
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Verify HBSP-Proto’s core claims of (i) higher accuracy, (ii) sharper calibration, and (iii) automatic rank discovery on real images.

Protocol
1. Backbone & Training: Pre-train ResNet-12 and ViT-S/16 on the meta-training split of miniImageNet and tieredImageNet (15 epochs, SGD, same schedule for all methods).
2. Episodic Evaluation:
   • Standard: 5-way {1, 2, 5}-shot, 15 queries/way, 10 000 episodes on miniImageNet-test and tieredImageNet-test.
   • Cross-domain: train on miniImageNet-train, test on CUB & iNat-mini.
   • Shot-mismatch: train with 5-shot, test with 1-shot.
3. Methods compared: HBSP-Proto, ProtoNet, EST+L2, LR-AM, LaplacianShot, ProtoLP, STLR-Proto.
4. Metrics: Accuracy, NLL, ECE (15-bin), average chosen rank r_eff, episodic wall-time.
5. Hardware: single A100; batch size 1 episode to avoid leakage.

Expected Demonstration
• HBSP-Proto outperforms best baseline by +6 pp (1-shot) / +3 pp (5-shot).
• ECE < 2 % vs 8–10 % for others; NLL ↓15 %.
• r_eff adapts: 4 ± 1 on miniImageNet, 9 ± 2 on MedMNIST-Path, proving automatic rank selection.
• Wall-time within 1.1× ProtoNet ⇒ practicality.

Take-away: Confirms advantages A, C, D from the open-problem list on established tasks.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2 – Controlled Directional Shift Stress-Test
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Isolate the ability to detect and re-weight shifted feature directions (Open Problem B).

Protocol
1. Synthetic Generator: From the tieredImageNet validation features (frozen backbone), create domain-shifted copies by adding a rotation R_k that perturbs only the top-k PCA directions (k ∈ {5, 20}). Noise magnitude σ chosen so that vanilla ProtoNet accuracy drops ≈10 pp.
2. Episode Composition: Each episode contains support from the clean domain and queries from the shifted domain (simulates test-time domain shift).
3. Methods: HBSP-Proto with/without transductive spectral update, ProtoNet, LR-AM, LaplacianShot.
4. Metrics: Query accuracy, cosine overlap between predicted active subspace Û_y and ground-truth rotated subspace, Δr_eff before/after transductive update, run-time.
5. Hardware: run 1 000 episodes on 8 × A100 in parallel (map reduce).

Expected Demonstration
• HBSP-Proto (with update) recovers ≥80 % of the perturbed subspace directions (cosine ≥0.8) and regains 90 % of clean accuracy; others stay >6 pp lower.
• Without update the gap shrinks to 3 pp ⇒ proves benefit of one-step E-step.
• r_eff increases exactly by k when k≪d, showing on-the-fly reweighting.

Take-away: Direct evidence that spectral Bayesian machinery pinpoints and compensates for directional domain shift.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 3 – Safety-Critical Abstention & OOD Detection
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Validate HBSP-Proto’s Bayesian abstention head (Open Problem C).

Protocol
1. In-domain vs OOD: Use miniImageNet-test as in-distribution; Places-365, SVHN and Gaussian noise as OOD sets (all resized to 84×84).
2. Deployment Setting: Sequential stream of images; the model must either predict a class or “reject”. Threshold τ tuned on a 5 % held-out split and fixed afterwards.
3. Methods: HBSP-Proto (tail integral), STLR-Proto (scalar Student-t), ProtoNet + temperature scaling, MSP baseline.
4. Metrics: AUROC, AUPR-out, FPR@95 %TPR, selective-risk curves (coverage vs error), expected calibration error at each coverage, and compute overhead.
5. Hardware: batched inference on 8 × A100, 1 million images total.

Expected Demonstration
• HBSP-Proto achieves AUROC 0.90 (↑12 %) and FPR@95 %TPR ≈20 % (↓40  %) over best baseline.
• Selective-risk: at 80 % coverage error rate halves (3 %→1.5 %).
• Overhead <7 % vs ProtoNet.

Take-away: Shows that marginalising over the precision matrix yields trustworthy confidence useful for safety-critical rejection, fulfilling requirement C.

--------------------------------------------------
Combined Conclusion
Together, the three experiments attack accuracy, adaptability, and safety from orthogonal angles. Success across all of them would convincingly demonstrate that HBSP-Proto’s hierarchical Bayesian spectral metric is an all-round improvement over current few-shot and transductive metric-learning techniques.
