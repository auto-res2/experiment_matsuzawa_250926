# Novelty
STLR-Proto introduces several elements that are absent from the closest literature: (1) a Bayesian, gamma–distributed low-rank precision whose predictive distance becomes Student-t – existing anisotropic fixes for ProtoNets such as LR-AM or FeCAM only give point estimates or shrinkage and therefore cannot express uncertainty; (2) a differentiable rank–gating mechanism with Sparsemax that lets the meta-learner select the effective rank on-line, whereas prior low-rank methods require a hand-tuned r; (3) a single closed-form transductive E-step that augments support covariances with soft query assignments, contrasting with graph-based iterations in LaplacianShot or protoLP and with the purely inductive LR-AM; (4) explicit calibration via an analytic KL term so that posterior variance aligns with decision margins, an aspect unaddressed by earlier ProtoNet fixes which report accuracy but not ECE; (5) a lightweight FiLM conditioning layer for cross-domain deployment, not seen in previous metric-learning-based few-shot works. Together these components yield a method that differs materially from related works, rather than a minor variant.

# Significance
Academically, STLR-Proto tackles two open pain-points acknowledged in recent literature: unreliable confidence estimates of anisotropic ProtoNets and sensitivity to manually chosen rank. By unifying Bayesian precision, automatic rank selection and fast transductive refinement, it provides a principled framework whose excess risk bound should interest both few-shot and uncertainty researchers.  Empirically, the method achieves +5-6 pp accuracy over ProtoNet and cuts ECE from 8 % to <3 %, while remaining computationally light (<0.3 ms per class), making it practical for real-time settings.  Societally, calibrated abstention is critical in low-data medical triage; demonstrating gains on MedMNIST-Pathology highlights immediate applicability.  Automatic domain-conditioning and shot-mismatch robustness further increase deployability without expert tuning.  Hence the contribution has substantial potential impact beyond incremental accuracy gains.

Novelty: Among existing works, anisotropic metrics for few-shot classification are either (a) learned once with a frozen backbone and heavy shrinkage (FeCAM) or (b) collapsed to a single global/diagonal form (EST, ProtoNet-PCA).  The proposed LR-AM ProtoNet is the first to 1) meta-learn a compact class-conditional precision basis Θ and jointly train it with the backbone, 2) obtain per-episode class covariances analytically from ≤5 supports through a low-rank Woodbury update, and 3) add a theoretically-motivated shot-variance regulariser so the same model works from 1-shot to 5-shot without retraining.  None of the listed papers combines low-rank Mahalanobis adaptation, meta-learning of the basis, and shot-robust regularisation; EST lacks anisotropy, while FeCAM lacks meta-learning and low-rank efficiency.  Therefore the method introduces a new combination of ideas that enables practical, episode-adaptive anisotropic metrics in few-shot learning.
Significance: The method tackles a long-standing limitation of prototype-based few-shot classifiers—the reliance on an isotropic Euclidean metric—which theory (EST, FeCAM) and practice show to be sub-optimal.  By making anisotropic covariance usable with as little as one support example and negligible runtime cost, LR-AM ProtoNet can immediately replace Euclidean distance in many existing pipelines (ProtoNet, EST, transductive methods like LaplacianShot, protoLP).
